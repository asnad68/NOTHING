 # VERQIVIA — Commercial Pilot Framework

## Purpose

The pilot is the bridge between the current public prototype and a possible commercial product. It should test one concrete business problem with a limited scope and measurable outcomes.

## Intended pilot participants

The first participants should be organizations that have a practical need to make business identity and authorization information easier for people and software to inspect.

Examples include:

- businesses with several official digital channels;
- marketplaces that need a structured business identity reference;
- software platforms that need machine-readable verification records;
- service providers that need to distinguish verified relationships from self-claims.

No participant should be described publicly as a customer, partner or endorser until a written agreement authorizes that statement.

## Suggested pilot scope

A first pilot can be limited to:

1. one organization;
2. one Nothing ID;
3. up to five business claims;
4. a small set of supporting evidence;
5. one or more versioned verification procedures;
6. one public or controlled verification page;
7. API output for machine inspection, where appropriate;
8. a short review period and written feedback.

The exact scope, data categories, security controls and commercial terms must be agreed before processing non-public information.

## What the pilot should measure

The goal is evidence about product utility, not a marketing vanity metric.

Measure:

- time required to understand a business identity;
- time required to verify a selected claim;
- usefulness of explicit evidence and procedure history;
- ease of machine integration;
- number and type of verification failures;
- privacy and data-minimization requirements;
- operational effort required to maintain the record.

## Commercial model

The repository does not yet define a fixed price. During early pilots, pricing can be agreed per project after scope is known.

A commercial proposal should state:

- pilot scope;
- deliverables;
- timeline agreed by the parties;
- fee and payment terms, if any;
- data handling;
- responsibilities of each party;
- acceptance criteria;
- confidentiality requirements;
- intellectual-property treatment;
- termination terms.

Cryptocurrency settlement remains deferred until its legal, compliance, custody, reconciliation and operational requirements are independently cleared.

## Public claims policy

Until a participant gives written permission:

- do not publish its name as a customer;
- do not publish its logo;
- do not publish screenshots containing non-public information;
- do not describe a pilot as certification;
- do not claim regulatory approval or endorsement.

A pilot result should be described narrowly and factually.

## Recommended first outreach

The strongest opening is a concrete problem statement rather than a generic technology pitch:

> We are testing a transparent business identity and verification layer that separates identity, claims, evidence and verification procedures. We are looking for a small number of organizations willing to test one real identity-verification workflow and give structured feedback.

The public demo is available through the project website and repository. Any live pilot should use its own agreed data and controls.
